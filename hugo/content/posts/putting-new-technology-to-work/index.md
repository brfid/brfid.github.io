## Research question

How have changes in tasks, responsibilities and coordination helped people make better use of new technology?

Agentic workflows raise this question today as we decide how to divide tasks, share information and check results across people and AI agents.

This collection is an experiment in using Astra to gather historical cases and leads relevant to that question.

This review summarizes 229 collected case accounts and leads, arranged first by sector and then by the kind of work change. Each appears once, under its main mechanism; some involve several mechanisms or sectors.

Reported benefits sit alongside mixed, adverse, disputed and incomplete accounts. Incomplete lead marks a specific missing connection or unread account, not merely an absence of numerical measurement. Unmarked entries describe a reported benefit or a useful bounded comparison; they do not certify a separate causal effect of work redesign. Several entries share a study or source family, so this is not 229 independent studies or successes.

Public source links are included where a suitable source or publication record was identified. Transcriptions, abstracts and publication records are labeled where used; a link does not imply that every part of the linked work was read. Company and participant reports retain their attributed scope.

## Factory production and engineering

### Layout, routing and the sequence of production

#### Electrification

Individual electric motors let factories arrange machines and material movement around production flow rather than drive shafts. Contemporary operators reported greater output from the resulting combination of motors, handling and layout. [Devine, “From Shafts to Wires” (1983)][devine].

#### Baldwin Locomotive Works electric-drive handling

Electric motors, cranes and compact handling tools supported a new arrangement of equipment and material handling. Baldwin's operator judged that abandoning it would require more labor and floor space; this was his counterfactual assessment. [Contemporary engineering discussion (1901)][crocker].

#### Government Printing Office electric drive and layout

Electric drive allowed presses to move beyond a congested shaft-driven room into former storage space. The operator reported 15–20% more output per square foot with the same help, with added presses and infrastructure also contributing. [Contemporary engineering discussion (1901)][crocker].

#### CMN Delta CNC manifold line

A U-shaped layout, one-piece flow and handling aids reorganized work around installed CNC machines. Reported daily output rose from nine to twelve manifolds while direct operators fell from three to one; support labor and technical changes were not fully counted. [Alwazir’s field-study thesis (2000)][alwazir].

#### CMC order-by-order compressor production

Incomplete lead (technology–flow link): moving toward order-by-order production reportedly shortened complete-compressor lead time from 22 weeks to seven and then four. The contribution of the installed flexible-manufacturing equipment is underdescribed, and outsourcing and other plant changes accompanied the improvement. [Alwazir’s field-study thesis (2000)][alwazir].

#### EBG pump-housing planning sequence

Moving planning to the shop floor and removing successive approval stamps reportedly cut pump-housing processing from six weeks to two days. Incomplete lead (technology link): the account does not tie this flow improvement precisely to a particular flexible-manufacturing operation. [Alwazir’s field-study thesis (2000)][alwazir].

#### Bull Belfort availability-based assembly routing

Coded trays, component kitting and dynamic storage let work go to the first eligible available assembly station. Fiore reported reduced manufacturing delays, without a matched total-output or labor comparison. [Fiore (1987)][fiore].

#### Thomson Angers mixed-model television routing

Barcode-based routing and parallel preparation sent each television model to the first free station able to perform its next operation. Fiore attributed shorter production delays to this arrangement. [Fiore (1987)][fiore].

#### IBM Boigny flexible robotic assembly

Addressable pallets and asynchronous robot stations allowed operations to be regrouped and free equipment reused across programs. The contemporary account attributed productivity benefits to this flexibility, but its different product-mix output figures do not measure a common-period gain. [Fiore (1987)][fiore].

#### Volkswagen Hall 54

Difficult vehicle variants were diverted from automated flow into preparation and two-person assembly boxes, then returned to the conveyor. Jürgens credited a substantial local efficiency contribution; the extra box and logistics work remained part of the system.

#### Opel and GM Europe carrier-served assembly islands

Driverless carriers served stationary cockpit and door assembly islands, allowing longer, variable task cycles away from the main line. Mixed: the historian reported efficiency improvement alongside adverse worker reactions and no expansion of discretion.

#### Moorestown mixed-component single-line assembly

Pin-in-paste/reflow technology allowed surface-mount and through-hole work to use one line instead of looping between two. The survey reported 40% less cycle time and less rework, with materials and process tuning changing too. [Moorestown BMP survey (2001)][bmp2001].

#### Fujitsu Siemens Augsburg just-in-time cleaning removal

Electronic ordering and shorter storage supported just-in-time assembly deliveries, removing the need to clean housings before assembly. The account reported elimination of that task and about 1,000 litres of cleaner annually. [Behrendt, Würtenberger and Fichter’s company cases (2003)][izt].

#### Toyota Kyushu coil and axle preparation

Workers attached coils to axles before a combined installation stage using locally developed physical aids. A plant-visit account reported greater task efficiency, with some preparation work transferred upstream.

### Broader operator responsibilities, teams and maintenance

#### Jubilee Mill automatic weaving

Teams combined tending and routine maintenance around newly introduced automatic looms, with broader jobs, coordinated coverage, training and pay. Loom efficiency and cloth quality improved, but other groups improved too and later deterioration and repair qualify the explanation. [Rice’s weaving experiment (1953)][rice].

#### NOBØ Hommelvik panel-line work redesign

Broader teams, rotation, training and planning responsibility reorganized work on a locally new heater-panel line. Investigators reported 20% more acceptable panels per labor-hour, while equipment, staffing and demand also changed. [Emery and Thorsrud, Democracy at Work (1976), transcription][democracy].

#### Hunsfos chemical-pulp quality and coordination

Broader responsibilities, laboratory feedback and coordination across cooking, bleaching and shifts addressed demands of a new magnesium pulping process. Investigators reported cleaner, stronger pulp and fewer very poor batches, with some later retreat and concurrent technical changes.

#### Akzo Nobel Hengelo salt-processing task groups

Task groups took technical work, training and feedback responsibilities around automated packaging and logistics. Packaging efficiency reportedly rose 20% over 1994–96; shifts, maintenance and other inputs also changed. [De Leede’s field-study thesis (1997)][deleede].

#### Philips Roermond ceramic multilayer actuators

Operator mini-companies and action teams connected production with development as a ceramic-actuator process moved from the laboratory into manufacturing. The account reported less rejection and rework, process savings and quicker implementation of improvements. [De Leede’s field-study thesis (1997)][deleede].

#### Fokker Papendrecht FMS work and flow

Broader skills, task rotation and programming and quality support closer to production accompanied flexible-manufacturing equipment. Participants credited greater productivity and flexibility, and an eight-to-three-day lead-time goal was reached. [Alwazir’s field-study thesis (2000)][alwazir].

#### GE Salisbury circuit-breaker production teams

Teams took responsibility for quality, scheduling and work rules around automation and computer-generated production instructions. Contemporary reporting described higher productivity, shorter deliveries and less backlog, with longer hours and plant/product changes also involved.

#### Motorola Austin teams and factory-floor information

Operator teams used redesigned factory-floor information and took quality, scheduling and improvement responsibilities with technical support. Interview-based reporting described more monthly devices with half the direct workforce and fewer defects; continuous operation and support work qualify that comparison. [Miller’s strategy and training study (1990)][miller].

#### Renault mixed production and maintenance teams

Production and maintenance workers combined quick repairs with root-cause and reliability work in robotic body shops. Mixed: the historian reported reliability improvements and staff reductions, followed by weakened participation as workers feared losing their jobs.

#### Volkswagen AUTO 5000 Touran operation

Teams rotated tasks, solved problems and integrated maintenance, quality and logistics around new Touran production, including automated sections and laser welding. Researchers and managers reported productivity, quality and flexibility gains; critics challenged the evidence and the treatment of training and labor inputs.

#### Toyota Kyushu Miyata assembly design

Segmented conveyors, understandable equipment and ergonomic aids were combined with coherent group tasks, local checking and operator suggestions. Engineers reported 10% greater production efficiency than a conventional comparison, with the denominator and engineering contribution incompletely specified. [Hisada and Ota’s Toyota engineering account (1997)][hisada].

#### Toyota understandable automation and worker improvements

Operators used their understanding of engine/chassis and tire-installation automation to improve installed equipment. Availability began high and later exceeded roughly 99.5%; commissioning, engineering and maintenance also contributed.

#### SA television final assembly

Workers and managers improved material control and maintenance, bypassed failed stations and broadened some repair and programming roles around automated television production. Manufacturing time per television fell, but plant-wide figures cannot isolate individual line changes or increased work intensity. [Freyssenet’s automation study][sa].

#### Swedish assembly teams and specialist operators

Teams operated and improved mechanized line segments, with specialist operators and engineer coaching; retrospective accounts reported higher productivity and running time. Incomplete lead (adoption link): the particular locally new technology and its connection to this redesign remain unclear.

#### Automotive work systems and direct and overhead labor

Comparisons of assembly plants associated multiskilling and broader setup, maintenance and inspection responsibilities with labor outcomes after accounting for automation. Incomplete lead (adoption episode): the study supplies comparative context rather than a particular introduction and demonstrated technology–work interaction.

#### Steel finishing

Within-line comparisons associated teams, communication, incentives and broader jobs with higher uptime and better prime yield. Incomplete lead (adoption episode): the work evidence is useful, but the records do not identify a particular new-equipment episode.

#### Norsk Hydro new fertilizer plant

Broad operation, maintenance and cleaning roles, training and skill-based pay were designed before commissioning an automated fertilizer plant. Accounts reported low staffing and startup downtime relative to conventional plans or expectations, rather than an operated previous arrangement. [Emery and Thorsrud, Democracy at Work (1976), transcription][democracy].

#### Norsk Hydro old fertilizer plant

Workers analyzed and implemented staffing and maintenance rationalization at the older plant, including transfers to the new plant. Incomplete lead: the locally new technology and comparable output or quality benefit remain unclear. [Emery and Thorsrud, Democracy at Work (1976), transcription][democracy].

### Setup, diagnosis, feedback and incremental task improvements

#### MacroMed flexible-manufacturing setup

Operators and mechanics prepared before shutdown and performed independent setup tasks in parallel around a flexible-manufacturing cell. Mean setup downtime reportedly fell from 87.9 to 28.6 minutes, with technical adjustments contributing too. [Austin’s MacroMed thesis][austin].

#### CMC FMS cylinder machining and checking

Operator groups used paid problem-solving time to change machining sequence and check cylinders before the next operation. The field account reported correction of a specific defect, alongside technical changes and group support. [Alwazir’s field-study thesis (2000)][alwazir].

#### CMN K-block quality program

Prevention, measurement, review and continuous improvement accompanied flexible machining and coordinate measurement. Reported defects fell from 19.9 to five per hundred cylinder blocks across the wider production operation. [Alwazir’s field-study thesis (2000)][alwazir].

#### Moorestown robotic phase-shifter cells

Operators checked quality and statistical process measures during robotic phase-shifter assembly. The survey reported over 60% less touch labor and less handling damage and variation, excluding some maintenance and backup work. [Moorestown BMP survey (1995)][bmp1995].

#### Moorestown UV acrylic protective end coating

A worker added protective end coating while applying an installed UV-cured acrylic coating to component sides. The account reported less chip damage; earlier faster curing came from the material change rather than this suggestion. [Bridger and Ruiz’s phase-shifter case study (2006)][bridger].

#### Ontario aerospace cutter-use monitoring

Condition and use logging replaced a fixed rule of changing drilling cutters after 200 holes. The field account reported 220–230 acceptable holes per cutter, avoiding some changes without establishing a plant-wide productivity gain. [Nicholson’s aerospace field-study thesis (2023)][nicholson].

#### Quebec aerospace composite-layup robots

Workers joined suppliers and engineers in debugging, programming and maintaining new composite-layup robots. The field account reported rapid error reduction and improved operating capability, without measuring final-skin quality separately. [Nicholson’s aerospace field-study thesis (2023)][nicholson].

#### Quebec aerospace drilling and fastening

Selected operators learned diagnosis and program adjustment while helping debug drilling and fastening robots. Mixed: management credited better accuracy and one participant recounted a fault fixed in minutes, but most workers saw no reduction in hours per join. [Nicholson’s aerospace field-study thesis (2023)][nicholson].

#### Ontario aerospace virtual joining

Methods and assembly workers remeasured parts locally, revised supplier-based virtual models and planned joining together. The field account reported progressively fewer errors and workable joining, with extra measuring and modeling integral to the solution. [Nicholson’s aerospace field-study thesis (2023)][nicholson].

#### VELUX production data and operator coaching

Managers learned to coach operators in using installed real-time production data to investigate line problems. Researchers reported 10% greater line performance over four weeks, without defining it as labor productivity. [Saabye, Kristensen and Wæhrens (2020)][velux2020].

#### VELUX pane feeding and short-stop work

Operators used pane-line production data to investigate short stops and change feeding and workflows. A practitioner account reported 8% higher line productivity, without a labor denominator or raw series. [Saabye and Powell’s practitioner account (2024)][velux2024].

#### VELUX flashing and cladding line comparisons

Daily coaching and data-based problem solving accompanied reported productivity growth on a flashing line; a separate IoT cladding line reported higher equipment effectiveness. These are different line comparisons and metrics, and cladding's coaching chronology is unspecified. [Saabye, Kristensen and Wæhrens (2022)][velux2022].

#### Motorola statistical quality and worker problem-solving

Workers were trained and rewarded to diagnose and fix manufacturing problems alongside statistical quality methods. Incomplete lead (technology link): company accounts reported sharply fewer defects, but the particular new technical tool or equipment connection and consistent outcome definitions remain unclear.

#### JMS garment production and sewing quality

Layout, specialized tasks, training and shifts changed around new garment-production machinery. The company/consultant account reported more pieces per worker-day and lower sewing rejection over separate periods. [DEG’s JMS company/consultant account (2016)][jms].

#### TINE Heimdal packaging-line work

Teams coordinated breaks, shifts, next-shift preparation, label changes and troubleshooting around packaging machinery. Mixed; incomplete lead (adoption link): maintained deliveries and productivity gains were recounted alongside pressure and an eventual hours-arrangement reversal, but the particular new equipment or technical demand is unidentified.

### Engineering collaboration and information handoffs

#### HP Lake Stevens expert-assisted design for manufacture

Engineers combined expert design software with manufacturing feedback to simplify products, including using snap connections. Contemporary reporting described fewer failures and less manufacturing time across thirty products, with design and tool changes integral.

#### Motorola Florida pager development and order response

Cross-functional teams coordinated new pager and automated-factory development through deadlines and schedule discipline. Contemporary reporting described faster development and a separate shorter order-to-shipment capability; neither result isolates the work contribution.

#### Motorola MicroTac design and robot assembly

Teams coordinated phone design across departments and produced the new MicroTAC using robot-assisted assembly. Assembly was reported at two hours versus forty for 1985 phones, but different product generations and omitted engineering inputs prevent a total-labor comparison.

#### Moorestown CAD/CAM electronic handoffs

Networked CAD/CAM linked design and production information and replaced some paper and tape transfers. The survey reported a productivity gain with an undefined denominator; complete paperlessness remained a goal. [Moorestown BMP survey (1995)][bmp1995].

#### Moorestown CAE methods and cross-discipline work

Cross-functional engineering teams standardized methods, tools, training and early collaboration around computer-aided engineering and rapid prototyping. The survey reported task-effort reductions in documentation, circuit-board design and prototyping, without a reliable combined aggregate. [Moorestown BMP survey (1995)][bmp1995].

#### Moorestown designer finite-element analysis

Designers used simpler finite-element tools and shared geometry to perform iterative analysis earlier, retaining specialists where needed. One program estimated $13,000 in process-labor savings, with overlap with other model trials unknown. [Moorestown BMP survey (2001)][bmp2001].

#### Moorestown microwave simulation and engineer roles

Broadly skilled engineers used microwave simulation for analysis formerly divided among theorists and programmers, while retaining physical validation. The survey reported shorter development and a task-labor comparison of two work-years versus six work-months, excluding some software and model development. [Moorestown BMP survey (1995)][bmp1995].

#### Moorestown shared parametric 3D models

Sites and suppliers reused a common parametric model instead of translating and recreating geometry. Two trials reportedly saved $28,000, while broader rollout remained planned and model/tool inputs were not totaled. [Moorestown BMP survey (2001)][bmp2001].

#### Moorestown web manuals and simultaneous review

Internal teams, customers and subcontractors reviewed technical documentation simultaneously through a shared web environment. The survey reported lower cost per page, faster delivery and fewer deficiencies at distinct scopes. [Moorestown BMP survey (2001)][bmp2001].

#### Moorestown COTS backfit land testing

Teams prepared and tested ship upgrades at a land facility near specialist resources rather than aboard ship. Three systems were successfully installed and operated, with reported labor cost 50% lower; facility capital and matched scope were not established. [Moorestown BMP survey (2001)][bmp2001].

#### Moorestown wirewrap local interactive processing

Workers replaced remote batch output with local interactive post-processing around existing automated wirewrap production. The survey credited this interaction with the largest throughput gains, while software and products changed too. [Moorestown BMP survey (1995)][bmp1995].

#### Moorestown electronic change root-cause review

Cross-functional teams regularly reviewed electronic change notices, required root-cause explanations and tracked corrections and recurrence. The company-derived account credited fewer changes and rework, shorter cycles and combined savings and cost avoidance. [Bridger and Ruiz’s phase-shifter case study (2006)][bridger].

### Planning, records and administrative work within production

#### Moorestown MRP materials data and shared ownership

Common ownership and integrated materials planning replaced independently operated materials functions. The survey reported markedly better bill-of-material and material-disbursement accuracy, alongside training, integration and oversight. [Moorestown BMP survey (1995)][bmp1995].

#### Moorestown ICAPS electronic provisioning

Contractors, reviewers and the Navy exchanged electronic provisioning information rather than preparing and transferring parts lists through older routines. A 3,000-item list reportedly required about 245 rather than 282 person-hours, excluding some wider support. [Moorestown BMP survey (1995)][bmp1995].

#### eltromat proALPHA master-data ownership

After old records had been imported into ERP without substantive checking, staff cleaned and standardized them and restricted master-data access to five people. The company reported markedly fewer duplicates and better data quality. [Frick’s eltromat case (2009)][eltromat].

#### eltromat ERP complete purchasing procedures

Staff followed complete ERP purchasing procedures and stopped direct-to-supplier shortcuts, sometimes adding steps locally. The company reported substantially less process time and fewer errors overall. [Frick’s eltromat case (2009)][eltromat].

#### ENGEL ERP, barcode routines and monthly reporting

Local procedures were adapted to standard ERP workflows, followed by barcode routines and revised monthly closing. The company reported fewer errors and a sales close on the second rather than roughly twenty-fifth day at distinct stages. [Myrach’s ENGEL case (2009)][engel].

#### Finzelberg electronic interdepartmental workflows

Role-based electronic workflows and invoice scanning replaced paper handoffs and clarified responsibility across departments. The company reported substantially shorter processing and easier collaboration, without an invoice-specific measured saving. [Schöpp’s Finzelberg case (2009)][finzelberg].

#### Fujitsu Siemens weekly consensus planning

Cross-functional staff replaced monthly planning with weekly consensus and electronic coordination using supply-chain software. Company reporting described better forecast accuracy, delivery-date adherence and inventory turns, with other procurement and capacity projects contributing. [Behrendt, Würtenberger and Fichter’s company cases (2003)][izt].

#### European computer manufacturer demand-planning routines

Regional staff entered forecasts directly and planned more frequently using i2 software. Vendor-supplied testimony reported forecast error falling from 40% to 12%; definitions, resources and extrapolated scrap savings remain uncertain. [Behrendt, Würtenberger and Fichter’s company cases (2003)][izt].

#### Television and video manufacturer electronic planning

A vendor account reported a planning cycle shortened from four weeks to one, more inventory turns and fewer rebookings under electronic supply-chain planning. Incomplete lead (work-change link): the actual tasks, responsibilities and routines behind these results are underdescribed. [Behrendt, Würtenberger and Fichter’s company cases (2003)][izt].

#### German PPS ending parallel card records

Sustained persuasion led staff to stop maintaining duplicate card records alongside production-planning terminals. This removed a concrete duplicate task, without establishing its hours saved or inheriting another department's capacity figure.

#### German CIM departmental capacity accounts

An expert reported about 30% more departmental work with the same staff after production-planning, CAD and numerical-control integration. Incomplete lead (work-change link): particular changed routines are not identified for the capacity accounts, and hours and transferred work are undefined.

#### German CIM upstream drawing practices

CAD–NC integration prompted demands for more exact upstream drawings instead of downstream repairs. Incomplete lead (benefit link): employees reported better documents more broadly, but a result from this particular routine is unestablished and added concentration and exposed errors drew criticism.

### Operator authority and participatory problem solving

#### General Electric Lynn: numerical-control pilot

Operators took wider preparation, diagnosis, inspection, planning and scheduling roles around numerically controlled machines. Disputed: favorable utilization and scrap accounts conflict with other company evidence, and some reported gains were temporary.

#### Boston-area numerically controlled lathe section

A lathe team took wider scheduling, tooling and programming responsibilities with less supervision. Mixed: Noble reported lower scrap, absence and turnover and better morale, but the program was later cancelled amid disputed explanations.

#### German A1 flexible manufacturing and group work

Production islands, broader jobs and later adjustments accompanied a flexible-manufacturing pilot. Mixed: throughput time roughly halved, while scrap and overhead per order increased amid smaller batches and greater complexity. [Moldaschl and Schultz-Wild (1994)][moldaschl].

#### German F2 NC milling pilot

A manager learned NC programming firsthand during a slow, conflict-laden milling pilot. Incomplete lead: no definite operating improvement is established for that pilot. [Moldaschl and Schultz-Wild (1994)][moldaschl].

#### German F2 turning-group responsibilities

Groups gained broader responsibilities, training, rotation, negotiated goals and mediation, and reportedly exceeded initial operating targets. Incomplete lead (adoption link): the exact technology connection is unclear, while exceeding targets does not establish improvement over prior performance. [Moldaschl and Schultz-Wild (1994)][moldaschl].

#### German F2 electronics follow-on groups

Follow-on electronics groups took broader tasks, with qualitative quality and productivity gains reported alongside morale costs. Incomplete lead (adoption link): the particular new technology is underdescribed; a demanded price cut was not an achieved gain. [Moldaschl and Schultz-Wild (1994)][moldaschl].

#### German F2 workshop circles

Workshop circles operated after assurances against layoffs and downgrading, with better flow and quality reported. Incomplete lead (technology link): the relevant production technology is unidentified for this episode. [Moldaschl and Schultz-Wild (1994)][moldaschl].

#### Volkswagen workshop networks and quality responsibility

Workshop contact circles and broader checking responsibility were promoted around automated production. Incomplete lead: publicity asserted better quality without a clear same-product/site comparison or consistent adoption episode, and the original publicity remains unread.

### Implementation priorities and process reorganization

#### BBA: stalled production-equipment project

New managers reprioritized a troubled equipment project and reopened earlier choices. Incomplete lead: the particular machine, resulting work arrangement and concrete operating benefit have not been recovered.

#### General Motors: unidentified automation plant

A secondary referral alleged disappointing productivity and quality after investment while labor-management practices remained unchanged. Incomplete adverse lead: the plant and equipment are unidentified and no later intervention or recovery is verified.

#### MRP II withdrawal and reintroduction

An indexed account says a manufacturer withdrew MRP II software, reorganized processes and reintroduced it. Incomplete lead: readable inspected evidence does not explain the changed work or an actual operating benefit.

### Procurement and distribution around machine production

#### Automatic cigarette manufacture

Direct leaf purchasing, warehouses, sales staff and managed distribution accompanied automatic cigarette machines. Mixed; incomplete lead (work-benefit link): historical accounts describe machine-cost advantages and expansion alongside excess capacity and profit pressure, without establishing the additional operating contribution of those organizational changes. [Porter’s cigarette-industry history][porter].

## Agriculture

### Coordinating harvest, transport and preparation

#### Mechanical tomato harvesting

Onboard sorting, trailer transport, night shifts and changed field preparation accompanied mechanical harvesting and later electronic sorting. Historical accounts report harvest labor falling from 5.3 hours per ton by hand to 2.9 with early machines and lower later, with varieties and agronomy changing too. [Thompson and Blank’s harvest history (2000)][thompson].

#### Rice harvesting

Direct combining was coordinated with transport, mechanical drying and bulk storage, replacing binding, shocking and separate threshing. Historical typical-system accounts report markedly less harvest labor, with machinery, yields and varieties part of the combined change. [Thompson and Blank’s harvest history (2000)][thompson].

#### East Nicolaus combine and bulk-drying system

Immediate transport and controlled bulk drying supported direct rice combining; separately, an operator moved cleaning until after drying. Bainer recounted high rice quality and workable cleaning, but the individual routine cannot inherit the whole system's benefit. [Bainer’s rice operating account (1932)][bainer].

#### California rice moisture service in 1931

Farmers used a new moisture-testing service to decide when to cut and thresh under combine-based harvesting. Bainer reported earlier operations and better-quality rice among users, with testing work, weather and varieties also relevant. [Bainer’s rice operating account (1932)][bainer].

### Crew composition and movement around harvest aids

#### California strawberry conveyors and crew coordination

Field comparisons associated conveyors and coworkers' ability with more boxes per worker-hour. Incomplete lead (work-change link): a distinct coordination change was not observed, and more productive workers selected into machine crews.

#### Santa Luzia tomato-harvest aid crews

A tractor-drawn aid was operated with two seated pickers and a walking collector, compared with one-person aided arrangements. Mixed: more kilograms per picker-hour accompanied poorer first-pass recovery in the higher-density comparison, with tractor and return-pass labor omitted. [Sánchez and colleagues’ harvest-aid experiment (2006)][sanchez].

#### Lodi orchard platform height and speed coordination

Automated platform height and speed adjustment removed the front picker's speed-control task in a two-picker crew. Short comparisons reported higher kilograms per hour, but conditions and source discrepancies limit precise interpretation.

#### Lompoc strawberry transport robots and tray requests

Pickers requested robot transport and exchanged trays with support from a station worker. Assisted sessions had higher productive-time fractions than estimated manual-transport references, excluding robot and support inputs from that picker measure.

## Forestry

### Preparing material for the next machine or stage

#### Finnish harvester bunching and downstream loading

Harvester operators varied pile size and arrangement for downstream forwarder loading. Field loading times differed by up to about forty seconds per cubic metre, without netting the upstream bunching work. [Väätäinen and colleagues’ loading study (2006)][vaatainen].

#### Black Forest bunching and mechanized thinning

Manual felling and winch bunching prepared stems for a tethered harvester. Null/mixed: no adjusted overall harvester-cycle advantage was found over standing-tree work, while upstream preparation incurred costs. [Berendt and colleagues’ field comparison (2020)][berendt].

#### South African Howe-line and prechoking sequence

Crews prechoked the next load while the current load travelled and reset lines at the landing using compatible tackle. Short trials reported higher extraction capability than a regional survey, with different terrain, hardware and support inputs.

### Loading positions, travel and machine-control routines

#### Rottne F10 forwarder loading positions

An experienced operator loaded logs under different machine/log arrangements on a stationary forwarder apparatus. The least favorable arrangement took 75% longer than the reference, demonstrating a narrow crane-cycle comparison rather than a field-wide gain. [Hartsch’s operator-practices thesis (2023)][hartsch].

#### John Deere forwarder loading positions and controls

A forwarder operator varied log position with boom-tip assistance and cab controls on or off. With aids on, one distant, angled position took 49% longer than the reference; one operator's task comparison does not establish a whole-operation gain. [Hartsch’s operator-practices thesis (2023)][hartsch].

#### Swedish harwarder loading and travel methods

One operator varied travel direction, processing side and direct loading with the same combined harvester-forwarder. The favored method achieved 13.0 cubic metres per effective hour against 12.1 and 11.9, in a small trial excluding delays. [Andersson and Eliasson’s work-method trial (2004)][andersson].

#### Swedish harwarder fixed and rotating carrier methods

An operator narrowed the strip to load more directly with a fixed carrier, trading loading benefits against travel. Mixed comparison: thinning output differed between carrier arrangements, while clear-felling and stem-damage differences were not significant. [Wester and Eliasson’s machine/method comparison (2003)][wester].

#### Lidhult Valmet Combi thinning trial

Direct loading, rehandling, travel and visibility shaped work with a combined harvesting/forwarding machine. Mixed/adverse economics: the trial attained output and low residual-stem damage, but did not demonstrate a damage improvement and modeled cost exceeded separate machines.

### Practice, instruction and learning to operate

#### Ås miniature yarder practice

Participants repeated winch-control practice over two days on a one-third-scale yarder. Control timing and tension consistency moved toward an experienced operator's performance on that narrow experimental task.

#### Wes Hood yarder training and field routines

Instruction changed directional felling, delimbing, payload and landing routines around a newly adopted yarder. The adjusted delay-free comparison was illustrated as 18.8 to 23.7 tons per productive machine-hour, with instructor time and stand changes qualifying attribution. [Haynes and Visser’s yarder account (2001)][visser].

#### Mont-Laurier simulator preparation and forest work

Training classes added staged simulator practice before real-machine forest work. Later cohorts had higher mean output and reported lower repair and maintenance costs, but additional practice and cohort and stand differences prevent a clean simulator effect.

#### Guangxi yarder learning and interrupted operation

Training and repeated use developed operating routines for a new yarder. Mixed/adverse: initial independent operation and modest task improvement coexisted with low utilization, interruptions and a later slight decline; proposed further integration was not implemented. [Hoffmann and colleagues’ yarding study (2016)][hoffmann].

### Shared planning, rotation and operating schedules

#### Gehren mechanized forestry teams

Crews shared planning, contacted foresters directly, rotated work and trained around harvester/forwarder operations. Participants reported better plan adherence and separate output and task-hour changes, with equipment, machine hours and team size also changing.

#### Forestal Mininco extended machine schedules

Different operators worked longer machine days and double shifts with harvesters and processors. Mixed: daily production generally rose while output per measured work-hour fell, with seasons, stands, lighting and maintenance differing. [Passicot and Murphy’s schedule comparison (2013)][passicot].

## Mining

### Flexible crew responsibilities and continuity across shifts

#### British semi-mechanized coal mining

Multiskilling, common pay and flexible task continuity replaced rigid divisions around electrical cutting and conveyors on semi-mechanized longwalls. Comparative faces reported 3.5 versus 5.3 tons per manshift, with geology, haulage and pay qualifying the comparison. [Trist and colleagues, Organizational Choice, publisher record][trist].

#### US underground coal-mine autonomous-group experiment

Crews broadened skills and integrated support around continuous miners and shuttle cars, while foremen concentrated on planning and safety. Mixed/disputed: some production-day comparisons favored autonomous sections, but evaluator Goodman judged overall productivity gains slight and later benefits weakened.

## Freight, ports and warehouses

### Coordinating handling across firms and transport stages

#### Container shipping

Compatible containers and handling equipment were combined with through-movements, standards, terminals and labor arrangements across ship, road and rail. Historical accounts report faster handling, less damage and shorter transit from this combined system. [Bernhofen and colleagues’ containerization paper][container].

#### Traditional-ship unitization and hatch work

Crews used prepared unit loads and forklifts in modified traditional-ship holds. A participant recounted four rather than eight hatch workers and loading in less than one-third the time, excluding shoreside preparation and other workers.

#### Pacific Coast dockwork and mechanization agreements

Mechanization agreements relaxed repeated-handling and manning rules around new cargo-handling equipment. Covered dockwork productivity rose in historical comparisons, with capital, cargo mix, off-dock preparation and worker protections integral to interpretation. [Goldberg’s longshore modernization account (1973)][goldberg].

#### New York longshore mechanization and work rules

Smaller gangs, flexible equipment assignments and transfers across hatches and ships accompanied mechanized terminals. General-cargo tons per covered man-hour reportedly rose from 0.434 to 0.686 between 1964–65 and 1970–71, with some labor shifted off-pier. [Goldberg’s longshore modernization account (1973)][goldberg].

#### Northwest ore-unloading crew rule

An agreement reduced the required basic gang around ore-unloading cranes from ten to two. Incomplete lead: implementation and actual post-change output are undocumented, so the contractual change is not an observed 80% productivity gain.

#### Fujitsu Siemens merge-in-transit distribution

EDI-supported carrier networks consolidated components in transit instead of routing everything through a central point. Company reporting described 10–20% less volume-distance and avoided trips on some routes, without totaling outside consolidation work. [Behrendt, Würtenberger and Fichter’s company cases (2003)][izt].

#### LeShop frozen-food delivery consolidation

Cold boxes, dry ice and adapted handheld/software logistics supported separate cold picking, cross-docking and revised customer handoffs and returns. One delivery replaced two, while cold-store, outsourcing and return-handling work remained. [Wölfle’s LeShop case (2009)][leshop].

#### Schindler SLOPE logistics coordination

Standard transport units and electronic logistics messages connected a carrier to purchasing operations. The company reported elimination of manual coordination on that route, but only one of three carriers was connected and partner/interface work remained. [Tanner’s Schindler account (2009)][schindler].

#### Digitec electronic shipment lists and delivery reliability

Electronic shipment lists passed parcel data directly to the postal service instead of relying on optical address capture. Next-day delivery reportedly rose from 97.5% to 99%, with the observation window and full carrier inputs unspecified. [Leimstoll’s Digitec case (2009)][digitec].

#### Refrigeration: Swift's beef distribution

Refrigerated rail carriage, branch cold storage and telegraph communication supported centralized slaughter and coordinated perishable distribution. Incomplete lead (additional benefit): freight advantages and a working arrangement are documented, but a concrete spoilage or handling improvement from the work changes is missing. [Fields’s Swift historical study][swift].

Warehouse-management software, racks and forklifts were combined with zoning, demand-based slotting and revised picking routes. IS staff worked beside forklift operators to design electronic location directions and scan confirmation, replacing paper picking after ERP implementation. #### NIBCO ERP demand-based warehouse slotting

After warehouse systems stabilized, staff repeatedly analyzed recent demand and moved busy items into accessible bins. The account reported less travel and greater productivity, without netting re-layout labor. [Brown, Tatikonda and Vessey’s NIBCO case (2003), transcription][nibco].

#### NIBCO ERP cycle counting

Selected plants used cycle-counting routines with installed ERP inventory records and, in some comparisons, RF equipment. Shorter counting and better accuracy were reported, but the exact counting sequence is underdescribed and the original publication images remain unavailable. [Brown, Tatikonda and Vessey’s NIBCO case (2003), transcription][nibco].

#### Variosystems ERP and warehouse recording

Staff adopted common ERP processes and barcode-directed storage and retrieval. The company reported consistently locatable materials and revenue growth absorbed without more personnel, which are different claims rather than one physical-productivity measure. [Stöckler’s Variosystems case (2009)][variosystems].

Suppliers checked shared requirements and stock information four times daily and managed replenishment. [Behrendt, Würtenberger and Fichter’s company cases (2003)][izt].

NIBCO assumed customer replenishment decisions using EDI, joint implementation and recurring policy reviews. #### IBM production procurement and component matching

Shared procurement data, regular component-set negotiations and supplier-owned consignment stocks coordinated production supply. #### Benetton replenishment and linked production information

Sales, computer/CAD and factory information were linked with barcoded and robotic warehousing to coordinate reorders, production and distribution. Contemporary reporting described replenishment capability uncommon in the industry, without a net staffing or labor comparison.

Semi-automatic bus refuelling allowed interior vacuuming to overlap filling. Mixed: the operating account described workable overlap and satisfaction alongside longer bay occupation and higher maintenance costs, without a net staffing saving. [Robert’s RATP operating account (1990)][ratpfuel].

Robotic return-time filling reportedly removed the need for a dedicated refuelling agent at that stage. Mixed/adverse economics: two years of satisfactory operation were reported, but capital and vehicle-adaptation costs led to abandonment. [Robert’s RATP operating account (1990)][ratpfuel].

A sealed nozzle system could have allowed drivers to refuel and passed a technical test. Incomplete lead (deployment): maintenance arrangements, vehicle modifications and nozzle concerns blocked adoption, so no realized work benefit follows. [Robert’s RATP operating account (1990)][ratpfuel].

Integrated order, inventory and production information let sales staff reserve stock and confirm repeat orders immediately. Order software checked completeness while staff handled exceptions before fulfillment. The company/provider account reported markedly fewer errors and wrong deliveries, with hardware and training changing too. [Adolphs’s ad AUGROS case (2009)][augros].

Staff removed unnecessary collection and paper handoffs and retrieved customer information remotely using customer-relationship software. #### British-Swiss Chamber of Commerce membership portal

A membership portal combined self-service, consolidated classifications and externally operated systems in place of directory production and scattered records. #### Ford Europe order-bank dealer routine

Dealers searched an electronic order bank and modified suitable vehicles already in production instead of starting new orders. [Behrendt, Würtenberger and Fichter’s company cases (2003)][izt].

#### Regional Bell call-center teams and call delivery

Teams shared sales goals and solved customer, product and software problems together under a new call-delivery system. Comparative: team membership had a larger association with sales under that system, without establishing physical productivity or a quality interaction. [Batt’s call-center study (1999)][batt].

#### NIBCO electronic orders and service-center work

Service-center routines were standardized while customers increasingly used web and electronic-data ordering after ERP implementation. Fewer staff were reported, but volume, mix and customer/vendor work prevent treating the staffing figure as matched productivity. ### Delegated purchasing and division of work with suppliers

#### HP Germany delegated catalogue purchasing

Staff could place low-value catalogue orders with one less approval layer, local delivery and local acknowledgement. Pilot participants reported order-to-delivery falling from about a week to usually 48 hours. [Renner and Schwengels’s electronic-commerce case studies (2000)][renner].

#### Mannesmann desktop purchasing and receiving work

Preselected suppliers, fewer low-value approvals and some automated receiving accompanied desktop purchasing, while staff moved into catalogue work. Participants reported substantial goods-receipt relief; the large process-cost reduction remained a forecast. [Renner and Schwengels’s electronic-commerce case studies (2000)][renner].

#### German telecommunications firm desktop procurement

Employee ordering and monthly supplier billing replaced a longer low-value purchasing process. Managers reported nine steps becoming three and cost per order falling from DM 160 to DM 65, without a physical-productivity measure. [Renner and Schwengels’s electronic-commerce case studies (2000)][renner].

#### UBS electronic procurement and outsourced supply work

Departmental representatives ordered electronically while outside partners took supply and logistics work, with monthly billing. An early participant account reported about half the document volume and roughly 65 internal positions progressively saved, without a net-chain labor measure. [Lüthy’s UBS procurement account (2002)][ubs2002].

#### UBS electronic-invoice approval routing

Process ownership and designated employee/approver routes handled electronic invoices lacking order references. The account reported markedly faster handling; supplier formatting, fees and coordination were inputs and uptake missed its target. [Tanner’s UBS invoice account (2004), author edition][ubs2004].

#### UBS central paper-invoice scanning

Central outsourced scanning, checking and routing brought paper invoices into an existing electronic workflow. #### Baxter companion: electronic ordering and hospital supplies

Electronic hospital ordering expanded into supplier-managed stockrooms, inventory and replenishment. [Short and Venkatraman, original-publication abstract][baxter].

#### Kaved and Schindler integrated cabling orders

Kaved took production and logistics responsibilities and linked customer cabling specifications directly to manufacturing orders. Customer time per order reportedly fell below one-third, with new facilities, interfaces and supplier data work contributing. [Leimstoll’s Kaved study (2005)][kaved].

#### Sterling Heights multi-tier production planning

Shared planning software linked bills of material and transmitted production plans across four supplier tiers. Company testimony reported transmission falling from fourteen days to under twenty-four hours, without totaling supplier and development inputs. [Behrendt, Würtenberger and Fichter’s company cases (2003)][izt].

#### Barcodes and retail supply chains

Machine-readable product identifiers supported scanning and information exchange, and a manufacturer panel found adoption-related growth but no statistically detected revenue-productivity gain. Null; incomplete work-change link: that study did not observe a concrete inventory or replenishment redesign, and its revenue measure need not reveal physical gains. [Basker and Simcoe’s barcode study][upc].

#### ManB SAP open-item selection

A user selected a range of about 1,000 SAP open items instead of selecting each one separately. The respondent estimated an hour saved each month, a small individual benefit distinct from hypothetical wider extrapolations. [Staehr, Shanks and Seddon (2003)][staehr].

The construction group restructured reporting paths and adopted single data entry with a new enterprise resource planning system. #### Weiss+Appetito master-data cleanup and maintenance

Staff removed duplicate and obsolete ERP migration records and assigned maintenance to trained employees under accounting controls. Incomplete lead (benefit comparison): cleaning 6,000 addresses and 20,000 contacts in about two weeks records work performed, not time saved; extra part-time and student work also contributed. [Semar’s Weiss+Appetito case and master-data extension][weiss].

#### Zach CompC ERP capacity and repricing

Participants described system-wide repricing within minutes and sales growth absorbed without hiring after ERP adoption. Incomplete lead (work-change link): the precise revised routines are underdescribed, while changing prices and sales mix limit productivity claims.

#### NIBCO ERP cross-functional service review

Cross-functional groups repeatedly investigated service failures, reviewed common ERP dashboard measures and linked incentives to service goals. Students combined acquisitions and cataloguing and followed items through the process using Sirsi and netLibrary. Identification and labeling were directed through a new barcode cutting-bench workflow. Staff changed retrieval and dose-scanning routines around barcode-assisted dispensing, including carousel and two-day-fill configurations. Pharmacy leadership responded to technicians' concerns by supplying two rather than three days of medication under the new barcode workflow. Ward and pharmacy tasks were reorganized around electronic prescribing, decision support and automated unit-dose supply introduced together. Nurses developed routines and confidence with automated unit-dose supply after initially duplicating checks. Staff routed empty cytostatic pouches around an automated repair line and filled them manually in a separate step. #### SSA decision templates and writing assignments

Structured software prompts, smaller and more frequent assignments and monitoring changed decision drafting. Mixed: drafts per writer-day rose over the reported period with a dip, while quality testimony raised concerns about unsupported boilerplate rationales. [SSA decision-writing audit (2010)][ssa2010].

#### SSA National Hearing Centers

Dedicated judge–writer relationships, fuller preparation and different staffing accompanied video hearings and electronic preparation. Centers reported 2.77 dispositions per judge-day versus 2.42 nationally in FY2011, with selected cases and exported work limiting system-wide inference. [SSA National Hearing Centers audit (2012)][ssa2012].

### Remote working arrangements

#### ATO corporate-services remote work

An already dispersed team moved individually assignable work home using existing information systems after redeployment. The official account reported roughly 25% more output per remaining worker, with slightly longer hours, so this was not an hourly-productivity gain. [Australian Public Service Commission account (2019–20)][apsc].

#### ATO citizen-services remote review work

About 180 staff conducted two-stage reviews from home with system lag, fewer screens and printers and harder consultation. Adverse: the stages reportedly took twenty rather than sixteen hours, a distinct operation rather than a control for the corporate-services team. [Australian Public Service Commission account (2019–20)][apsc].

### Matching postal staffing, stations and startup routines to equipment

#### US Postal Service automation and workforce controls

Workforce-complement controls later adjusted staffing around mail-processing automation. Mixed: GAO attributed part of a larger 1990 improvement to the controls alongside workload and equipment, but subsequent performance reversed. [GAO postal productivity review (1992)][gao1992].

#### USPS Atlanta small-package sorter work

Staffing matched volume and supervisors managed setup and bin handling around automated parcel sorting. Mixed comparative account: the site outperformed four selected low sites in 2017, but labor coding weakened the comparison and later results fell below targets. [USPS Inspector General sorter audit (2017)][usps2017].

#### USPS Columbus small-package sorter startup

A process-improvement project standardized sorter startup, assignments, training and labor coding. Mixed/adverse follow-up: the site exceeded a target in 2017, rather than demonstrating a measured before/after gain, then deteriorated with idle stations and positioning, supervision and labor-recording problems. [USPS Inspector General sorter audit (2020)][usps2020].

#### USPS San Jose small-package sorter stations

Staff used all five induction stations, started on time and held meetings around an automated sorter. Recorded packages per employee-hour exceeded the national comparison, with some labor charged elsewhere and extra stations potentially requiring more people. [USPS Inspector General sorter audit (2020)][usps2020].

## Utilities and environmental services

### Team responsibility for process and quality

#### TransWater wastewater teams and environmental quality

Teams took broader plant, shift, procedure and quality responsibility amid wastewater automation and outsourcing. A chapter reported 11–30% improvements in externally monitored pollutant measures; incomplete lead (technology detail) and related-sample uncertainty limit the adoption explanation. [Cordery’s Australian work-teams chapter (2000), publication record][cordery].

#### Wastewater autonomy and production uncertainty

An abstract reported better treated-effluent quality after team autonomy, with related reporting suggesting larger gains under high uncertainty. Incomplete lead: the particular new technology, full methods and relationship between reported samples remain unresolved. [Cordery and colleagues (2010), abstract][wastewater].

## Software development, IT support and equipment service

### Integrated development tools and changed development work

#### MidCo: computer-aided software engineering

An integrated software-engineering methodology and toolset replaced limited standalone-tool use alongside changed development work. Incomplete lead (benefit): uptake was enthusiastic, but a senior manager said benefits were being taken on faith rather than recounting a realized improvement. [Gallivan, Hofman and Orlikowski’s implementation study (1994)][midco].

### Process ownership, escalation and reusable solutions

#### Croatian financial-services ITIL implementation

Process owners, escalation, configuration management, reusable solutions, training and audit accompanied new IT-service tools. Involved authors reported shorter resolution and more first-level fixes, with changed coverage and an unresolved contradictory change-control row. [Spremić, Žmirak and Kraljević’s ITIL case (2008)][itil].

#### Saudi mental-health survey helpdesk knowledge reuse

Helpdesk staff recorded incidents, searched prior solutions and used manuals and escalation while building a ticket-system knowledge base. Mixed: median elapsed resolution fell from twenty-five to 12.5 hours between phases, but one priority group worsened and experience and case mix also changed. [Aradati and colleagues’ helpdesk study (2019)][aradati].

#### Xerox France Minitel Eureka

Technicians organized repair tips, rapid peer validation and recognition around a Minitel knowledge system. Designers reported better diagnosis, fewer escalations and faster learning, with content, software and training changing together. [Bobrow and Whalen’s Eureka account (2002)][eureka].

#### Xerox France Colombus field-tip comparison

Workshop stories became validated searchable tips that technicians consulted on laptops during repairs. A matched two-month comparison reported about 10% less service time and parts cost, without fully counting tip-production work and with possible comparison contamination. [Bobrow and Whalen’s Eureka account (2002)][eureka].

#### Xerox US individual Eureka routines

Technicians consulted searchable practical tips before calls or early in diagnosis rather than saving them for last resort. Interviewees recounted faster work, with laptop and search burdens remaining and no transfer of the French trial's percentage to these US routines. [Bobrow and Whalen’s Eureka account (2002)][eureka].
